## Plot summary 
_This Place: 150 Years Retold_ is an anthology of eight comics about the Indigenous people of Canada. The book moves chronologically, starting with Annie Bannatyne, a Métis woman who horsewhipped Canadian nationalist Charles Mair in 1869. One story depicts the life of Francis Pegahmagabow, a World War I sniper who became the most decorated Indigenous solder in Canadian history. Another tells of a 1980 salmon raid, when Quebec Provincial Police stole the livelihood of the Mi’gmaq people.

Each comic is introduced with an author’s note and a timeline of major events that frame the story. The book is published as fiction, but serious historical research informs the work, which ends with a bibliography.

## Critical evaluation
_This Place_ defies the idea that graphic novels make for light reading. Though some of the drawings feel formulaic or haphazard, others pierce through the page. Either way, the stories are so powerful that they sat with me for days. 

I learned about Inuit psychology and shamanism, the defeat of the Mackenzie Valley Pipeline, and the devastating Sixties Scoop that allowed the Canadian government to place Indigenous children in foster homes. But _This Place_ is not a boring history book. Every character came to full life, even in their short, 20-page stories.

It’s hard to put a genre label on this book. “Red Clouds” and “Rosie” read like true horror. The closing story, “kitaskînaw 2350,” is set in a dystopian future. But even the more standard histories and biographies, like “Peggy” and “Tilted Ground,” hold an eerie, mind-bending quality. Indigenous people have been living in a post-apocalyptic world for hundreds of years, Chelsea Vowel writes (Akiwenzie-Damm et al., 2019). So, maybe some genre bending — even genre breaking — is fitting. _This Place_ is a “refusal to lose hope, and a denial of oblivion.”
