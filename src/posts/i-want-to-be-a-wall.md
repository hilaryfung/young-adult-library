## Plot summary 
_I Want To Be A Wall_ begins on Yuriko and Gakurouta’s wedding day. Their setup is unusual: Yuriko is an asexual woman who enjoys boys’ love comics, and Gakurouta is a gay man in love with his oblivious childhood friend. The three-volume manga series follows their lavender marriage through comedic mishaps and a growing bond. Translation notes are included.

## Critical evaluation
_I Want To Be A Wall_ is a breezy read. Yuriko and Gakurouta have never been in relationships, nor lived alone, so they struggle to do laundry, buy groceries, and make pancakes that don’t turn into charcoal. The book is about societal expectations, but it’s not so serious. Yuriko and Gaku collaborate on a cover story for their marriage, deciding which bedroom they’ll pretend they share when a prying family member asks. Yuri cowers under all-caps text — STRANGE FEELINGS OF OBLIGATION — when she thinks about doing household chores for her husband.

My favorite parts were the childhood flashbacks, when Yuriko shares how she felt defective until a university classmate told her about asexuality, and Gaku tells her about the day he decided to keep his love for his best friend private. Slowly but surely, their awkward communication turns into a heartwarming story of acceptance and understanding.

## Library program
_Yuriko’s escape._ Yuriko likes to escape into the world of BL, where she never has to be reminded of society’s expectations for women. When she introduces the genre to Gaku, he likes it for an entirely different reason. Serve pancakes or a similar comfort food at the library while screening a boys’ love series like _Sasaki and Miyano_ (Chester-Londt, 2026). Yuri (girls’ love) anime like _Bloom into You_ is an option, too.

## Speed-round book talk
Yuriko and Gakurouta have an unusual marriage. Yuriko’s an asexual woman obsessed with boys’ love comics, and Gakurouta is a gay man in love with his childhood best friend. This lighthearted three-volume manga series follows the growth of their comedic and heartwarming relationship.

## Potential challenges
_I Want To Be A Wall_ is not visually explicit, but thematically, it’s very queer. The entire series subverts societal gender norms; even Yuriko’s friend, a side character, enters the plot to talk about how she doesn’t want children. While I haven’t seen record of censorship attempts (Raedts, 2026), it’s a good idea to prepare to defend this title. It’s a positive example of open communication in relationships.

## Reason for inclusion
I don’t have much experience with manga, but a lot of people have told me it’s popular, so I wanted to check out the format for this collection. I also wanted to include a title with an asexual main character, something that’s still pretty rare in media (Allen & Jones, 2023).
