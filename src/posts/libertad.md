## Reading level and interest level
Ages 14 to 18, per [Kirkus](https://www.kirkusreviews.com/book-reviews/bessie-flores-zaldivar/libertad-3/).

## Plot summary 
It’s 2017. The right-wing National Party has governed Honduras for eight years. In a looming election, Juan Orlando Hernández will vie for his third term as president, defying the constitution. Students protest at the National Autonomous University, also known as la UNAH, and military police open fire.

Against this charged political backdrop, Libertad Morazán turns 18. She worries about her brother, who gradually opens up to her about his involvement in the UNAH student movement. Libi worries about his safety, and about her friendship with Camila, which lost its closeness after the two girls kissed at a nightclub. She worries about her future, because as much as she loves her family, her hometown, and her country, she knows that staying in Tegucigalpa means she’ll face austerity, authoritarianism, and homophobia.

_Libertad_ is told primarily from Libi’s point of view, with a handful of flashbacks that show what her other family members lived through before she came of age. Libi’s poems, written in Spanish and translated to English in the back of the book, serves as points of emotional relief for the reader and for her community.

## Author background
Bessie Flores Zaldívar is a writer and professor (The Interlochen Review, 2025). Originally from Tegucigalpa, they now live in New Haven. Their debut novel _Libertad_ is “very autofictional,” they said in an interview.

## Critical evaluation
Published seven years after its true events took place, _Libertad_ is not quite a work of historical fiction. But it includes everything I love about that genre. Libi’s personal story is woven with major events of recent Honduran history, from the 2009 U.S.-backed military coup to the nationwide power outage that coincided with Juan Orlando Hernández’s 2017 election. As she watches her country change, Libi grapples with new love and family secrets. She’s young enough to need her mother’s acceptance and her brother Maynor’s protection, but she’s old enough to make big decisions. Will she forgive her best friend for not being ready to come out? Is her personal and political freedom worth leaving her family and homeland behind?

_Libertad_ contains nuanced politics and enough queer characters that no one person feels like a stereotype. Libi clashes with her reactionary mother and conservative grandparents, but she understands when Maynor explains how their views were shaped by their experiences. She admires Dani, an openly queer university student who’s only a little older but who carries herself with the confidence of a different world. She wonders if her aunt, killed in the 2009 wave of anti-LGBTQ state violence, was queer.

Fully set in Honduras, _Libertad_ holds parallels to the U.S., where authoritarianism is on the rise. At the same time, it tells a uniquely Honduran story in which the U.S. is both a meddler and an escape. Zaldívar revisits recent history with honesty, demonstrating their strength not only as a writer, but as a human. Her characters, too, struggle toward honesty and justice, while trying to balance patience and understanding. As Libi says, “What’s more revolutionary than that?”

## Library program
_Library art walk._ At UNAH, a public mural of los desaparecidos — students and activists who have gone missing — helps Libi’s family heal. Curate works of public art and display them in your library. You can get creative here: Ask patrons to submit work, or print out photos of murals, sculptures, mosaics, and other works of public art. Choose a mix of local work and pieces from other countries, including Honduras. 

Disperse the displays throughout your library as much as possible, then create a map to guide patrons on an “art walk” through the library. Include recommended reads with each display, and if you’re feeling ambitious, host a guided walking “tour” for teens.

## Speed-round book talk
Libertad Morazán turns 18 at a tense moment in Honduran history. An authoritarian president is defying the constitution to run for his third term, protesters are jamming the streets, and the police are gunning down student activists. Libi navigates first love, queer coming-of-age, family arguments, grief, and a big question: Will she leave the only country she knows and loves?

## Potential challenges
Queer identity and relationships are central to this novel. It might help librarians to know that though the main character has sex in the book, nothing is described. The book also covers themes of political resistance — not surprisingly, given the Molotov cocktail on the cover. The historical and political lessons are important parts of a civic education.

## Reason for inclusion
Though the events of _Libertad_ take place in Honduras, the U.S. is ever-present. So besides being an entertaining and heartbreaking read, this novel shows American teens how our interventionist policies affect other countries.
