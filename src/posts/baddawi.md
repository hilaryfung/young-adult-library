## Awards and honors
* Shortlist, Palestine Book Awards, [2015](https://www.palestinebookawards.com/books/2015)

## Reading level and interest level
Ages 13 and up. _Baddawi_ wasn’t published specifically for young adults, but it tells a coming-of-age story that teens will appreciate. It’s shelved as a teen book at my local library, and some have listed it as a children’s resource (Amalia team, 2023).

## Plot summary 
In 1948, Leila Abdelrazaq’s grandparents were living in Safsaf, Palestine. They fled Israel’s ethnic cleansing and landed in Baddawi, a refugee camp in northern Lebanon. Here, the author’s father Ahmad was born. _Baddawi_ tells the story of his life, from an UNRWA grade school to the American University of Beirut library, where he snuck in to study as a high schooler.

In graphic novel format, Abdelrazaq shows us elements of a normal boyhood — Ahmad gets bullied, then sets up a gambling scheme over marbles to buy new soccer cleats — alongside major historical events, like the 1967 Naksa and 1973 Israeli raids on Lebanon. The book closes with a glossary of terms and real photos of Ahmad and his family.

## Author background
Leila Abdelrazaq is a Palestinian author, artist, and organizer (Abdelrazaq, n.d.). She’s pursuing a Ph.D. in art history and practice at UC San Diego. 

## Critical evaluation
Abdelrazaq draws from rich Palestinian visual tradition in _Baddawi._ On the cover of the book, an illustration of Ahmad faces away from the reader, a nod to Naji al-Ali’s Handala. Tatreez patterns adorn the pages, honoring traditional Palestinian embroidery.

These powerful symbols convey the stakes that are always on Ahmad’s mind, even as he goes through the mundane tasks of teenage life. He plays chess on a board that blends into a keffiyeh pattern; he swims in a sea that swirls with bombs and warplanes. Abdelrazaq draws bold, black-and-white shapes with only a couple levels of hatched midtones, creating surreal drama without much graphic violence.

I’m most impressed by the story itself. Conversations between author and father (Scott, 2026) resulted in sharply detailed vignettes, and this oral history — this work of memory — makes _Baddawi_ an exemplar of the graphic novel format.

## Library program
_Family archive._ Teens are likely to carry a lot of memories on their phones’ camera rolls. Invite them to bring photos and other items of personal or family significance — print or digital —  to the library. Help them print photos and create personal archives, writing about each item’s significance in a scrapbook or blog.

## Speed-round book talk
In 1948, Leila Abdelrazaq’s grandparents fled ethnic cleansing in Palestine and landed in Baddawi, a refugee camp in northern Lebanon. Here, the author’s father Ahmad was born. _Baddawi_ tells his coming-of-age story in beautifully illustrated graphic novel format.

## Potential challenges
Books about Palestinian history and experience regularly attract challenges (Bird, 2022). _Baddawi_ should be defended, as it’s a valuable historical record that more than deserves its place in the library.

## Reason for inclusion
As the U.S. government works to stigmatize Palestinians (Ludwig, 2024) and restrict refugee admissions (Santana, 2025), it’s important for American teens to have access to historical context. _Baddawi_ provides a glimpse into the conditions that create statelessness, or that might cause a person to want to seek refuge. The book is highly accessible, including dates and definitions for unfamiliar audiences, but the highly personal look at life in the camps can be valuable for anyone.
